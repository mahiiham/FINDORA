How we protect your data

No jargon — just a plain explanation of what happens to your information when you use Findora.

Your password

We never store your actual password — not even we can see it. When you sign up, your password is run through a one-way scrambling process (called hashing). Think of it like a paper shredder: it turns "MyPassword123" into a long string of random-looking characters, and that shredding can't be undone. When you log in, we shred what you type and check if it matches the stored shreds — your real password never touches our database.

Your "secret proof" details

When you report a lost or found item, you can add a private detail only the real owner would know (like "there's a coffee stain inside the front pocket"). This is encrypted before it's saved — imagine locking it in a small safe. Only the app itself holds the key to that safe, and it's only opened for a moment during admin review, never shown on any public page.

Chatting with another user

When you message a claimant or a reporter, you never see their real name, email, or phone number — only a label like "Claimant" or "Reporter". We also automatically scan outgoing messages and blank out anything that looks like an email address, phone number, or social-media handle, so contact details can't accidentally slip through before the platform's official return process is complete.

Your location

Your device position is only read when you allow your browser to share it. It is saved with a report you file so you can see where you were, and the optional “remember my location for 6 hours” trail is visible only to you and deleted automatically after 6 hours (or when you press “Clear my trail”). Admins and other users never see it; the risk map shows only anonymous counts per campus place.

Who can see what

Public search pages only ever show what's needed to recognize an item — a title, description, category, location, and photo. Your private "secret proof" text, your exact contact details, and your account information are never shown to other regular users. Only admins can review sensitive claim details, and only while deciding a specific claim.

We keep a paper trail (audit log)

Every important action — a report created, a claim approved or rejected, an account role changed — is logged with a timestamp and who did it. This isn't visible to regular users; it exists so admins (and you, if something looks wrong) can always trace back what happened and when. It's like a security camera for actions, not for people.

How the "AI" parts work

The matching engine and the visual-similarity check run entirely on our own server, comparing text and photos you've already shared publicly (title, description, category, photo) — nothing is sent to an outside company or a third-party AI service. The "confidence score" shown to admins is just simple, explainable math (how similar two pieces of text are) — never a black box, and it never makes the final decision by itself.

In one sentence: we store the minimum needed to reunite people with their things, we lock away anything sensitive, we hide personal contact details during chat, and a human always makes the final call on approving a claim.
